# Backtesting protocol (design)

**Owner decision, 2026-10-20: minimum-data RPL pilot rather than 2015/16–2026/27 historical completeness.** The binding proposed scope is [M0 minimum RPL scope](M0_MINIMUM_RPL_SCOPE.json): **2024/25** (240 training fixtures), **2025/26** (240 sealed out-of-sample fixtures), and **2026/27** (current fixtures, subject to verified freshness and rights). These are *publisher-reported structural counts* only; no provider is approved or data imported. An older draft requested training 2015/16–2022/23 and validation 2023/24–2024/25; that distribution is retired, **not** a current requirement.

## What the minimum can legitimately establish

- Fit a **fixed simple baseline** (independent Poisson with conservative regularization and a pre-specified, shared home advantage) using only verified 2024/25 results and no later labels. The model's available per-club sample size is small: use robust prior/shrinkage and an honest insufficient-data fallback for promoted/unseen teams. No hyperparameter search or data-driven calibration is permitted on the sealed 2025/26 test.
- Produce chronological 2025/26 **retrospective evaluation** with fixed preprocessing, metrics (Log Loss, Brier, RPS), sample size, scoring convention and grouped uncertainty. Do not report that an apparently positive test proves predictive advantage. If genuine 2025/26 pre-match feature snapshots are unavailable, label the study **non-strict retrospective**, not an authenticated historic walk-forward simulation; never use the 2025/26 match's own outcome or subsequent season totals as its features.
- When legally approved and independently evaluated, a **new** operational model may train on both completed seasons, with current-season observations strictly available before each forecast as-of cutoff. The sealed 2025/26 benchmark remains a separately labeled historical evaluation, not a continually retuned test. All live forecasts must be published before kickoff with immutable prediction versions; none exists at M0.
- This two-completed-season minimum provides **no independent calibration/validation cohort**. M3 confidence in calibration, extensive hyperparameter tuning, Dixon–Coles variants, Elo comparisons, advanced ensembles and a publishable performance superiority claim require more independently licensed historical seasons **or** pre-registered prospective observations with separate validation/calibration/test windows. No rerunning dozens of variants against 2025/26 until a lucky winner emerges.
- If fewer than **two** fully legally reusable/verified completed seasons are available, stop at informational fixture/result displays (subject to rights), **not** a measured ML forecast. If 2026/27 is unavailable or stale, show explicit unavailable status; never fabricate a calendar or a probability.

## Temporal and source gates remain unchanged

For every historical prediction cutoff T, reconstruct only records demonstrably available by T; fit preprocessing exclusively on pre-T data. Distinguish evaluation labels (eventual official corrected results) from historical pre-match features. Present-day archive downloads without original publication and observed-at snapshots cannot prove historical pre-match availability. Do not fabricate strict as-of evidence from repository Git commits.

Preserve source record provenance, match/event/publication/observation/ingestion times, provider version, dataset/model digests, and chronological split IDs. Exclude administratively awarded 3-0 results from played-goals model fitting; preserve them separately for official standings and evaluation policy. Pre-register the baseline family, scoring rules, minimum sample sizes, season selection and bootstrap scheme **before** running any real fit. Do not use random match shuffles, look-ahead, or test-label tuning.

All provider rights, upstream ownership, storage, licensed training, commercial/public-derived display permissions and official fixture reconciliation gates still apply; this smaller scope is not permission to collect data. M0 and issue #2 remain OPEN.
